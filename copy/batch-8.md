What we don't do

[H2] What we won't do, and why that protects you

[P] Some roofers promise to "handle everything." In Virginia, only a licensed public adjuster can negotiate a claim settlement on your behalf ([Code of Virginia § 38.2-1845.1](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title38.2/chapter18/section38.2-1845.1/)). We document the damage and stand behind our estimate. You and your insurer agree the settlement.

[P] We also never pay or "waive" your deductible. Starting January 1, 2027, [Virginia Code § 59.1-618](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title59.1/chapter61/section59.1-618/) makes that illegal for contractors, and an offer like that is the clearest sign of a storm chaser. Our guide on [whether insurance covers roof replacement](/guides/does-insurance-cover-roof-replacement/) explains the new rules in full.

Timelines

[H2] How long your insurer has to answer

[P] Virginia sets deadlines for insurers in its claim settlement rules ([14VAC5-400](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title14/agency5/chapter400/)). Knowing them helps when a claim goes quiet.

[TABLE]
| Step | Virginia's deadline for the insurer |
| Acknowledge your claim notice | Within 15 calendar days (14VAC5-400-50 A) |
| Reply to your other claim questions | Within 15 calendar days (14VAC5-400-50 C) |
| Accept or deny, once you've sent a proof of loss | Within 15 calendar days, or tell you in writing why it needs more time (14VAC5-400-60 A) |
| Update you while still investigating | In writing every 45 calendar days (14VAC5-400-60 B) |
| Explain a denial | In writing, citing the policy provision it relies on (14VAC5-400-70 A and B) |

[P] If those dates slip, ask your adjuster in writing. If that goes nowhere, the [Virginia Bureau of Insurance](https://www.scc.virginia.gov/regulated-industries/bureau-of-insurance/) takes complaints.

FAQ

[H2] Insurance claim questions

[FAQ Q] What if the adjuster's estimate is lower than yours?

[FAQ A] It happens, usually because something was missed or measured differently. We show the adjuster the damage we documented, with photos and line items. The settlement is between you and your insurer. If you want someone to negotiate it for you, that has to be a licensed public adjuster.

[FAQ Q] My shingles aren't made anymore. Will insurance pay for a full roof?

[FAQ A] Sometimes. If new shingles can't match the damaged ones in size or look, a patch can leave you with a mismatched roof. We document the mismatch and show it to your adjuster. That's what turned John W Allen's patch into a full replacement in 2025. Your policy and your insurer decide the outcome.

[FAQ Q] Should I call my insurer or a roofer first?

[FAQ A] Stop any leak first. Then get an inspection before you file, if you can. It tells you whether the damage is real storm damage or age, and you file with photos instead of a guess. If the damage is small, a repair can cost less than your deductible.

[FAQ Q] Can you talk to my insurance company for me?

[FAQ A] We talk to your adjuster about the damage: we meet them on the roof, answer their questions and send our photos and estimate. We don't negotiate your settlement. Virginia law reserves that for licensed public adjusters.

[FAQ Q] How long does a roof claim take?

[FAQ A] Virginia gives insurers 15 calendar days to acknowledge your claim and 15 days to accept or deny it after a proof of loss, with written updates every 45 days if they need longer. Once the claim is approved, we schedule the work and give you a start date.

Permits

[H2] When Arlington requires a permit for a new roof

[P] [Arlington County's rule](https://www.arlingtonva.us/Government/Programs/Building/Permits/Residential) turns on the decking under your shingles. Replacing the shingles alone needs no building permit. Neither does replacing less than 256 square feet of decking. More than 256 square feet, and a permit is required.

[P] Townhouses are the big exception. If your roof was built with fire-retardant-treated plywood, which the code often required where townhouse roofs meet a shared wall, Arlington requires a permit no matter how little decking is replaced. We check for it at the inspection. Laying new plywood over the old decking is a separate case: the county asks you to call the Permit Arlington Center at 703-228-3800.

Historic districts

[H2] Historic districts and HOAs

[P] Arlington has local historic districts, Maywood among them, where exterior changes can need a Certificate of Appropriateness and the county can require a permit for new roofing even when one wouldn't apply elsewhere. [Maywood's Homeowner's Handbook](https://www.arlingtonva.us/Government/Projects/Plans-Studies/Historic-Preservation/Design-Style-Guides), on the county's historic preservation pages, explains when. If you live in a district or an HOA, send us the guidelines and we quote a roof that meets them.
