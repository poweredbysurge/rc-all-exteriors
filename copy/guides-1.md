[H1] Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Roof Replacement in Virginia?

[BYLINE] By Ricardo Castro, owner of RC All Exteriors, Virginia Class A Contractor #2705196138 · Published November 2026

Intro

[P] Usually yes, if wind, hail or a falling tree damaged the roof. Usually no, if the roof simply wore out. Virginia's homeowners policy rules require insurers to cover the house for direct physical loss from any cause, then let them exclude a short list of things. Wear and tear is on that list.

[P] This guide covers what that means for a roof, how a claim actually goes, and the new Virginia rules for roofers that start January 1, 2027.

Coverage

[H2] What insurance covers, and what it doesn't

[P] Virginia regulation [14VAC5-342-50](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title14/agency5/chapter342/section50/) requires homeowners policies to cover the dwelling for "open causes of loss." In plain terms: if something sudden damages the roof, it is covered unless the policy excludes it.

[P] The exclusions are in [14VAC5-342-60](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title14/agency5/chapter342/section60/). The ones that matter for roofs:

[LIST]

- Wear and tear and deterioration. An old roof shedding granules is maintenance, not a claim.
- Neglect. If you don't take reasonable steps to protect the house after damage, like tarping a hole, the extra damage can be excluded.
- Water that gets in without an opening. For your belongings, insurers can exclude rain damage inside unless wind or hail first made an opening in the roof or walls.

[P] What usually is covered: shingles torn off by wind, hail bruising, a tree limb through the deck, and the water damage that follows through that opening.

Repair or replacement

[H2] Repair or full replacement?

[P] Insurance pays to put the roof back to its condition before the storm, so a claim can pay for a repair, a section, or the whole roof. It depends on how much is damaged and whether the damaged shingles can be matched. Your adjuster decides that, based on what they see and what the policy says.

[P] Two policy terms drive how much you get. Replacement cost value pays what it costs to replace the roof today, usually after the work is done. Actual cash value subtracts depreciation for the roof's age. Your declarations page says which one you have, and what your deductible is. Some policies have a separate, higher deductible for wind and hail.

Claim steps

[H2] How a roof claim goes, step by step

[STEPS]

1. **Protect the house.** Tarp any opening and photograph everything first. This is the "reasonable means to protect the property" your policy asks for.
2. **Get a roof inspection.** We go up, photograph the damage shingle by shingle, and tell you honestly whether it looks like storm damage or age. If it's age, we say so, and you save yourself a claim on your record.
3. **Call your insurer and open the claim.** Ask for your claim number and your adjuster's name.
4. **Meet the adjuster.** We can be on the roof with the adjuster so the damage they see matches the damage we found.
5. **Get an itemized estimate.** Every RC estimate lists the work line by line, so you can compare it to the insurer's.
6. **Pay your deductible.** That part is always yours. See the next section for why.

New rules

[H2] New Virginia rules for roofers starting January 1, 2027

[P] Virginia's Residential Property Owners Protection Act (Code of Virginia [§§ 59.1-617 to 59.1-620](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacodefull/title59.1/chapter61/)) takes effect January 1, 2027. It protects homeowners from the storm-chaser tactics we see every season. Under it:

[LIST]

- A contractor cannot pay, waive or rebate any part of your insurance deductible (§ 59.1-618 A). An offer to "cover your deductible" is a red flag.
- A contractor cannot offer gifts, cash or credits in exchange for a roof inspection or for filing a claim (§ 59.1-618 B.2).
- A contractor cannot have you authorize repairs without a good-faith, itemized estimate (§ 59.1-618 B.4).
- Roofing contracts must carry specific notices, and if the deductible notice is missing, you can void the contract within 10 days of signing (§ 59.1-618 B.5).
- If you sign within 180 days of a governor's emergency declaration for your area, you can cancel within 10 days or by the start date, whichever comes first (§ 59.1-619).
- A contractor cannot act as your public adjuster. Only a licensed public adjuster can negotiate your claim for you (§ 38.2-1845.1).

[P] What that means with us: we document the damage, meet the adjuster, and give you an itemized estimate. We don't negotiate your settlement, and you always pay your own deductible.

FAQ

[H2] Questions homeowners ask us

[FAQ Q] Will my insurance pay for a roof that's just old?

[FAQ A] No. Virginia lets insurers exclude wear and tear and deterioration. If your roof is near the end of its life without storm damage, filing a claim won't change that. A free inspection tells you which situation you're in before you call your insurer.

[FAQ Q] Can a roofer pay my deductible?

[FAQ A] Starting January 1, 2027, no. Virginia Code § 59.1-618 makes it a violation for a contractor to pay, waive or rebate any part of your insurance deductible, and it is enforced under the Virginia Consumer Protection Act. Treat any offer like that as a warning sign.

[FAQ Q] Do I have to use the roofer my insurance company suggests?

[FAQ A] Your policy decides how claims are paid, so read it, but you can usually get your own inspection and estimate. Ask your adjuster how they want competing estimates submitted, and keep every estimate itemized so the line items can be compared.

[FAQ Q] How long do I have to file a roof claim?

[FAQ A] It depends on your policy, so check the conditions section or ask your agent. Report damage as soon as you can. Delays make it harder to show the damage came from a specific storm, and damage that gets worse while you wait can fall under the neglect exclusion.

[FAQ Q] Will one claim raise my rates?

[FAQ A] It can, depending on your insurer and your claim history. That's one reason to get an inspection first. If the damage is small or the roof is simply old, a repair paid out of pocket can make more sense than a claim.

Note

[NOTE] This guide is general information about Virginia rules, not legal or insurance advice. Your policy wording controls. For questions about your coverage, contact your agent or the Virginia Bureau of Insurance.

[H1] CertainTeed Landmark vs Landmark Pro: Which Shingle for Your Roof?

[BYLINE] By Ricardo Castro, owner of RC All Exteriors, CertainTeed ShingleMaster · Published November 2026

Intro

[P] Most of the roofs we put on in Northern Virginia are CertainTeed Landmark or Landmark Pro. Both are architectural shingles with a lifetime limited warranty and the same 110 mph wind rating out of the box. Landmark Pro adds longer algae protection, a bigger color range and broader early-years coverage. Here is how to choose.

Comparison

[H2] Side by side

[TABLE]
| | Landmark | Landmark Pro |
| Warranty | Lifetime limited | Lifetime limited |
| Wind | 110 mph, 15 years | 110 mph, 15 years. Up to 130 mph with CertainTeed starter and hip and ridge shingles |
| Algae (StreakFighter) | 25 years | 30 years |
| SureStart, first 10 years | Materials and labor | Materials, labor, tear-off and disposal |
| Colors | Traditional palette | Max Def palette, including Georgetown Gray, Weathered Wood, Driftwood and Moiré Black |

[P] Source: CertainTeed's Landmark Pro product brochure. Exact terms are in the warranty document for the shingle you buy.

Algae

[H2] Why algae protection matters here

[P] The black streaks on roofs across Woodbridge, Lake Ridge and Fairfax are algae, and it grows best on shaded, damp slopes under big trees. Both shingles use CertainTeed's StreakFighter granules, which contain copper to slow it down. Landmark Pro's algae coverage runs five years longer. On a north-facing roof under oaks, that's the difference we'd pay for.

When Landmark

[H2] When we suggest Landmark

[LIST]

- A rental or a house you plan to sell in a few years.
- A sunny roof with little tree cover.
- A budget where the money is better spent on decking or ventilation.

When Landmark Pro

[H2] When we suggest Landmark Pro

[LIST]

- A shaded roof where algae streaks are already showing.
- An HOA or a historic look that needs a specific color, like Georgetown Gray or Weathered Wood.
- A house you plan to stay in, where the broader early coverage and the 130 mph option are worth it.

[P] Either way, the shingle is only part of it. The underlayment, flashing and ventilation are where most roofs fail, so those get the same attention on every job.

ShingleMaster

[H2] What our ShingleMaster credential means

[P] ShingleMaster is CertainTeed's credential for contractors who complete its installation training. CertainTeed's Find a Pro directory lists RC All Exteriors as a ShingleMaster. CertainTeed's extended SureStart PLUS warranties that add workmanship coverage require its higher ShingleMaster PRO or PREMIER designations, so ask any roofer exactly which warranty they will register for you. We register SureStart PLUS 3-Star when you choose the extended warranty and we install the full CertainTeed system. On every roof, we add our own 10-year workmanship warranty.

From our jobs

[H2] From our jobs

[P] We quote Landmark and Landmark Pro on the spot at most inspections, because we install them every week. A recent full replacement used Landmark Pro over all-new decking, after we found the old plywood didn't meet current code.

FAQ

[H2] Questions homeowners ask us

[FAQ Q] Is Landmark Pro worth the upgrade over Landmark?

[FAQ A] If your roof is shaded or you plan to stay in the house, usually yes. You get 30 years of algae protection instead of 25, more colors, and SureStart coverage that includes tear-off and disposal. On a sunny roof you're selling soon, standard Landmark is a sound choice.

[FAQ Q] Do both shingles really have a lifetime warranty?

[FAQ A] Both carry a lifetime limited warranty from CertainTeed. "Limited" matters: full coverage applies during the SureStart period, then it changes. Read the warranty for the exact terms, and keep your registration paperwork with your house records.

[FAQ Q] Will Landmark Pro handle Northern Virginia wind?

[FAQ A] Both are rated for 110 mph wind for 15 years. Landmark Pro can be upgraded to 130 mph when it's installed with CertainTeed starter and hip and ridge shingles, which is how we install it when you choose the extended warranty.

[FAQ Q] Can I match my neighbors' color in an HOA?

[FAQ A] Usually. Landmark Pro's Max Def range covers most HOA-approved grays, browns and blacks. Bring your HOA's approved list to the inspection and we'll match it to a real sample before you sign.
